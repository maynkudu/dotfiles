# Avoid generic combinations

Source 2 in [source notes](source-notes.md) criticizes recurring combinations of
AI-generated design defaults. Its caption explicitly allows an individual pattern
when appropriate. Audit the composition and product meaning rather than enforcing
a blacklist of fonts, colors or visual techniques.

## Diagnose what the page communicates

- Decorative arrows on every action, unnecessary separators between words,
  repeated eyebrows, headline pills and numbered sections often add material
  without adding information. Follow the user's stricter rules for these elements.
- Cream surfaces, an italic headline word, a fashionable display serif, background
  glow, sparkle icons and dithering each need a reason tied to the brief. Combining
  them by default does not create a brand. Inspect contrast, readability and cost.
- Repeated cards and a default three-feature row can flatten unrelated content
  into the same shape. Use the structure the information needs. Keep cards when
  they represent real repeated objects, tasks or choices.
- A pulsing status dot must correspond to real state and convey useful information.
  Give status an accessible text equivalent. Keep a real status indicator when it
  helps; avoid decorative indicators and motion that competes with reading.
- Generic praise, repeated claims of authenticity, clipped slogans and fabricated
  metrics weaken evidence. Name a concrete benefit and show its actual proof.
- Blanket section fades hide useful content until a script and scroll trigger run.
  Choose motion for a specific relationship. Keep content usable without animation
  and with reduced motion, and check repeated interaction and loading performance.
- Colored callout rules and outlined cards remain subject to the user's no-border
  requirement. Preserve a user-approved exact copy and its documented exceptions.

## Correct the cause

Identify the generic element and the missing product information or relationship.
Change that element within the brief. A page with weak evidence needs evidence;
swapping cream for white or replacing one serif does not solve it. Keep a coherent
approved treatment instead of replacing every familiar pattern to appear original.

The source list is a critique prompt, not a statistical detector. Do not repeat the
video's percentages as research findings or label a design AI-generated from a
single visual pattern.
